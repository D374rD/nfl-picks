Reading the odds

  • Minus odds, like -115: the favorite side. You bet $11.50 to win $10.
  • Plus odds, like +170: the underdog side. A $10 bet wins $17.
  • If you win, you get your bet back plus the winnings.
  • "Better odds" means you win more for the same bet: a higher number for plus odds (+180 beats +170), or closer to zero for minus odds (-110 beats -120).

The three main bet types

  • Moneyline: pick who wins the game.
  • Spread: a team with + points can lose by less than that number and still win you the bet. A team with - points has to win by more than that number.
  • Total (over/under): bet whether both teams’ points added together go over or under the number.
  • Player props: bet on one player’s stats, like whether a receiver gets over or under 60.5 receiving yards.

Placing a bet

  1. Tap NFL. Each game shows the away team on top, the home team underneath, and three columns: Spread, Total, and Moneyline.
  2. Tap the button for the bet you want. It goes into your bet slip.
  3. Enter your amount as a single (straight) bet. If you add several picks, give each one its own amount. Leave the parlay box empty.
  4. Tap Place Bet. Your bets are listed under My Bets.

Terms to know

Kinds of bets

  • Single (straight bet): one bet on its own. It wins or loses by itself.
  • Parlay: several bets (called legs) joined into one bet. Every leg must win. If one leg loses, the whole parlay loses, even if the others all hit. The payout is bigger because it is harder to hit.
  • Same Game Parlay (SGP): a parlay with legs from the same game. The sportsbook pays less than the legs multiplied together, because the legs are linked (if a team has a big day, its players' overs tend to hit together) and it charges for that.
  • SGP+: several same-game parlays joined into one bigger parlay.
  • Round robin: the app makes several smaller parlays from your picks (for example every 2-leg combo of 4 picks), so you can still win something if a leg misses. Each small parlay is a separate stake.
  • Leg void: if a leg can't be settled (the player doesn't play, the game is postponed), it is usually removed and the parlay pays at the shorter odds of the legs left. Check your sportsbook's rules.
  • Push: a tie with the line (for example a spread of 3 and the team wins by exactly 3). A single is refunded; a pushed parlay leg is usually removed.

On the bet slip

  • Singles vs Parlay box: when you add several bets, the slip shows them as singles and offers a parlay. Any amount in a singles box is a separate bet. For a parlay only, leave the singles empty, put your stake in the parlay box, and check that the button says Place 1 Bet.
  • Stake (wager): the amount you bet. Payout is what you get back if it wins, including your stake; profit is payout minus stake.
  • Cash out: the sportsbook offers to settle your bet early for a set amount. It is usually priced in the book's favor.
  • Boost / profit boost: a promotion that raises the payout of one bet.

Lines and odds

  • Moneyline: pick who wins the game.
  • Spread (football) / puck line (hockey): the favorite must win by more than the number (for example -3.5); the underdog can lose by less than it (+3.5). The hockey puck line is almost always 1.5 goals.
  • Total (over/under): bet on whether both teams' combined points or goals go over or under a number.
  • Player prop: a bet on one player's numbers, such as rushing yards or shots on goal.
  • Alternate line / "X+" (ladder): the same prop at a different number, like 50+ or 100+ receiving yards. A higher number pays more and hits less often.
  • American odds: -114 means you risk $114 to win $100 ($10 wins $8.77). +150 means $100 wins $150 ($10 wins $15).
  • Implied chance: the chance the odds suggest. -114 is about 53%, +150 is 40%.
  • Vig (juice): the sportsbook's cut. Both sides of a bet add up to more than 100% (for example 53% + 53%), and the extra is the book's profit.
  • No-vig chance: the implied chances with the book's cut taken out, so they add up to 100%. This page compares the model to that.

How this page judges bets

  • Model chance: what the bot's model thinks will happen.
  • Edge: model chance minus the no-vig chance. Bigger isn't always better: huge edges usually mean news the model can't see.
  • Cautious chance: the model's chance pulled halfway back toward the market, because the model has been overconfident. Used for tickets.
  • Fair price: the odds a bet would need for the cautious chance to break even.
  • Expected points: from the spread and total together. A 47-point total with one team favored by 7 means about 27 to 20.
  • Closing line value (CLV): whether you got a better price than the final line before kickoff. Beating the closing line over many bets is the best early sign of a real edge; one win or loss means very little.
  • Bankroll / units: the money set aside for betting, and a fixed small slice of it per bet (for example 1%). Flat small stakes are the safest way to bet.

Long shots and ladder bets

For fun, not for the bankroll. These combine the bot’s own bets from different games into tickets. The “cautious” chance pulls each bet’s probability partway back toward the market, because the model is overconfident and mistakes multiply across legs. “Fair price” is what the ticket would need to pay for the cautious chance to break even. The model has not beaten the sportsbooks over time, so most of these will lose. Suggested stake: a few dollars at most. A parlay is one bet: the whole stake is on all the legs, and one miss loses it. Tap Add on each leg to put it in your DraftKings bet slip, then open the Parlay tab. Same-game parlays are not built here because the sportsbook prices the links between legs and this bot can’t see that price.

Oct 7, 2026 — College football, NBA and college basketball pages

  • New pages for college football, the NBA and college basketball (see the menu). They don't use a model: each DraftKings price is compared with Pinnacle, the sharpest big sportsbook, with its cut removed. When DraftKings pays more than that fair price, the bet has positive expected value and is listed.
  • Honest first result: on 54 college football games, DraftKings beat Pinnacle's fair price almost nowhere. Its prices cost about 4% on average (its cut). So the pages also show the bets that are Closest to a fair price, where you give up the least. They still cost a little on average.
  • NBA and college basketball fill in automatically once their seasons start (NBA later this month, college basketball in November).
  • To protect your Odds API credits, these pages only fetch odds when games are coming up and stop when fewer than 200 credits are left, so football always has credits.

Technical: market_run.py: one Odds API request per sport (h2h, spreads, totals; 10 books incl. Pinnacle = 3 credits), 60 min cache, free events check first, credit guard at 200. Fair chance = Pinnacle no-vig at the same line, else the average of at least 3 other books. Flags 1.5% to 10% value; 'closest to fair' shows -1.5% to 1.5%. Pages: cfb.html, nba.html, cbb.html; report.OTHER_SPORTS drives every menu. Daily 2 PM task via auto_run_sports.bat.

Oct 7, 2026 — Long shots and ladder bets: tickets, most likely legs, and more bet types

  • New Long shots and ladder bets section with three parts: a Most likely legs list (the single bets that look most likely to hit), Higher-chance tickets (from 3 up to 8 legs), and Long shots (2 to 3 legs picked for the biggest payout).
  • Tickets show the payout, a cautious chance to hit, what the model itself says, the fair price, and what $5 would win. Each has an Add button that loads the legs into your DraftKings bet slip; open the Parlay tab there.
  • New bet types: alternate-line X+ yardage (like 50+ receiving yards), passing TD ladders (1+, 2+) and anytime TD scorers, with the model's chance for each.
  • The cautious chance pulls every bet's probability partway back toward the market, because the model has been overconfident and errors multiply across legs. It will usually be lower than the model's own number, and the page shows how fast the chance drops as legs are added.
  • Alternate lines are fetched only for games starting within about a day, and only when there are enough Odds API credits left, so this section fills in closest to kickoff.
  • The model has not beaten sportsbooks over time, so most tickets will lose. This section is for fun with small stakes, not the bankroll. A parlay is one bet: the whole stake rides on every leg.

Technical: longshots.py builds tickets with itertools.combinations (one leg per game so legs are independent and the book's parlay price is the product). Probabilities are shrunk 50% toward the market's; each leg appears in at most 2 tickets; the higher-chance builder keeps the likeliest ticket for each size from 3 to 8 legs. extras.py reads alternate-line and anytime-TD markets from the Odds API (cached per game for 24h, 30h window, credit guard at 150), prices ladder rungs from the same player projections as the main props model (props.PROJ_CTX), and uses a Poisson model for passing TDs and a recency-weighted scoring rate, pulled toward a typical rate, for anytime TDs. Same-game parlays are not built because the sportsbook prices correlation and this bot can't see that price.

Terms to know

Kinds of bets

  • Single (straight bet): one bet on its own. It wins or loses by itself.
  • Parlay: several bets (called legs) joined into one bet. Every leg must win. If one leg loses, the whole parlay loses, even if the others all hit. The payout is bigger because it is harder to hit.
  • Same Game Parlay (SGP): a parlay with legs from the same game. The sportsbook pays less than the legs multiplied together, because the legs are linked (if a team has a big day, its players' overs tend to hit together) and it charges for that.
  • SGP+: several same-game parlays joined into one bigger parlay.
  • Round robin: the app makes several smaller parlays from your picks (for example every 2-leg combo of 4 picks), so you can still win something if a leg misses. Each small parlay is a separate stake.
  • Leg void: if a leg can't be settled (the player doesn't play, the game is postponed), it is usually removed and the parlay pays at the shorter odds of the legs left. Check your sportsbook's rules.
  • Push: a tie with the line (for example a spread of 3 and the team wins by exactly 3). A single is refunded; a pushed parlay leg is usually removed.

On the bet slip

  • Singles vs Parlay box: when you add several bets, the slip shows them as singles and offers a parlay. Any amount in a singles box is a separate bet. For a parlay only, leave the singles empty, put your stake in the parlay box, and check that the button says Place 1 Bet.
  • Stake (wager): the amount you bet. Payout is what you get back if it wins, including your stake; profit is payout minus stake.
  • Cash out: the sportsbook offers to settle your bet early for a set amount. It is usually priced in the book's favor.
  • Boost / profit boost: a promotion that raises the payout of one bet.

Lines and odds

  • Moneyline: pick who wins the game.
  • Spread (football) / puck line (hockey): the favorite must win by more than the number (for example -3.5); the underdog can lose by less than it (+3.5). The hockey puck line is almost always 1.5 goals.
  • Total (over/under): bet on whether both teams' combined points or goals go over or under a number.
  • Player prop: a bet on one player's numbers, such as rushing yards or shots on goal.
  • Alternate line / "X+" (ladder): the same prop at a different number, like 50+ or 100+ receiving yards. A higher number pays more and hits less often.
  • American odds: -114 means you risk $114 to win $100 ($10 wins $8.77). +150 means $100 wins $150 ($10 wins $15).
  • Implied chance: the chance the odds suggest. -114 is about 53%, +150 is 40%.
  • Vig (juice): the sportsbook's cut. Both sides of a bet add up to more than 100% (for example 53% + 53%), and the extra is the book's profit.
  • No-vig chance: the implied chances with the book's cut taken out, so they add up to 100%. This page compares the model to that.

How this page judges bets

  • Model chance: what the bot's model thinks will happen.
  • Edge: model chance minus the no-vig chance. Bigger isn't always better: huge edges usually mean news the model can't see.
  • Cautious chance: the model's chance pulled halfway back toward the market, because the model has been overconfident. Used for tickets.
  • Fair price: the odds a bet would need for the cautious chance to break even.
  • Expected points: from the spread and total together. A 47-point total with one team favored by 7 means about 27 to 20.
  • Closing line value (CLV): whether you got a better price than the final line before kickoff. Beating the closing line over many bets is the best early sign of a real edge; one win or loss means very little.
  • Bankroll / units: the money set aside for betting, and a fixed small slice of it per bet (for example 1%). Flat small stakes are the safest way to bet.
